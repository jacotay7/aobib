# Stroke saturation on a MEMS deformable mirror for woofer-tweeter adaptive optics

**Morzinski et al. (2009)** · *Optics Express 17, 5829* · [Free full text](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.17.005829) · DOI: [10.1364/OE.17.005829](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.17.005829)

## Summary

Measures empirically how often a high-order MEMS 'tweeter' saturates (runs out of stroke) when correcting atmospheric turbulence, in the context of the Gemini Planet Imager. A 1024-actuator MEMS mirror with 1.5 microns of stroke was tested in the laboratory with software Kolmogorov phase screens for r0 of 10-15 cm. On its own the MEMS saturated about 4% of the time, while adding a simulated woofer with about 5-10 actuators across a 5-m aperture reduced saturation to a fraction of a percent. Mid-to-high spatial frequencies saturated more often than expected, implying that influence-function correlations matter, and analytic models underpredicted the stroke requirements.
