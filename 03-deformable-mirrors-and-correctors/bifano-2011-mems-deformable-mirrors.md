# MEMS deformable mirrors

**Bifano (2011)** · *Nature Photonics 5, 21* · [Free full text](https://sites.bu.edu/bifano/files/2018/03/nphoton.2010.297.pdf) · DOI: [10.1038/nphoton.2010.297](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphoton.2010.297)

## Summary

A short 'industry perspective' article on microelectromechanical (MEMS) deformable mirrors. It traces the idea of shape-controlled mirrors from early history to Babcock's proposal of adaptive optics, then explains how MEMS mirrors are batch-fabricated from silicon wafers with thin-film processes, in continuous and segmented versions. Most examples are devices from Boston Micromachines Corporation, with 32 to 4,092 actuators, and the article stresses their cost, nearly an order of magnitude below the technology they replaced. It surveys applications that drove their development: extreme AO and space coronagraphy, AO for in vivo retinal imaging (scanning laser ophthalmoscopes and OCT), and biological microscopy, including multiphoton imaging.
