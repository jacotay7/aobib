# 03 · Deformable Mirrors & Wavefront Correctors

The wavefront corrector is the element that applies the adaptive-optics correction: a deformable mirror (DM), an adaptive secondary or quaternary mirror built into the telescope, a liquid-crystal spatial light modulator, or a transmissive adaptive lens. Its actuator count and pitch, stroke, bandwidth, linearity (hysteresis, creep), surface quality and reliability set the fitting error and much of the achievable performance, and they drive the design of the rest of the AO system. Technologies range from piezoelectric stack and bimorph mirrors, electrostatic MEMS and membrane devices, and magnetic (voice-coil and variable-reluctance) mirrors, to liquid crystals, ferrofluids and optically addressed devices.

This folder collects papers on corrector technologies and their characterisation: technology overviews and development programmes, the large-format voice-coil adaptive mirrors (MMT, LBT, VLT, GMT, ELT M4) and the newer hybrid-reluctance adaptive secondaries, MEMS and piezoelectric devices, novel correctors, influence-function modelling and open-loop control, woofer-tweeter architectures, and mirrors for space telescopes and coronagraphy. Control algorithms in general are in topic 04, and dark-hole wavefront control with DMs is in topic 07.
