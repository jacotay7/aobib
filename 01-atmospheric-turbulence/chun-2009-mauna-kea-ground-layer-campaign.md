# Mauna Kea ground-layer characterization campaign

**Chun et al. (2009)** · *MNRAS 394, 1121* · [Free full text](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article-lookup/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2008.14346.x) · DOI: [10.1111/j.1365-2966.2008.14346.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2008.14346.x)

## Summary

Reports an 18-month campaign measuring the vertical distribution of optical turbulence in the lowest kilometre above the summit of Mauna Kea with a combined SLODAR/LOLAS (low-layer SCIDAR) instrument on a small telescope. The boundary-layer turbulence is found to be confined to a very thin layer of 80 m or less, with weak turbulence between 80 and 650 m. The median free-atmosphere seeing is 0.42 arcsec, smaller than the typical delivered image quality of the large telescopes on the mountain. The authors conclude that local (near-ground and dome) seeing dominates and that ground-layer adaptive optics could be effective at the site; tube seeing, ground winds and possible biases in the data are also discussed.
