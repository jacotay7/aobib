# Optical Resolution Through a Randomly Inhomogeneous Medium for Very Long and Very Short Exposures

**Fried (1966)** · *JOSA 56, 1372* · [Free full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221725018_Optical_Resolution_Through_a_Randomly_Inhomogeneous_Medium_for_Very_Long_and_Very_Short_Exposures) · DOI: [10.1364/JOSA.56.001372](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.56.001372)

## Summary

Develops a theory relating the statistics of turbulence-induced wave distortion to the resolution of an imaging system, for very long and very short exposures. Resolution is expressed through the wave structure function, the sum of the phase and log-amplitude structure functions, and the long-exposure results agree with the earlier modulation-transfer-function analysis of Hufnagel and Stanley. The paper shows that short-exposure (tilt-removed) images can have notably better average resolution than long exposures, provided intensity fluctuations are small. It introduced the characteristic length now called the Fried parameter r0, the aperture diameter at which turbulence begins to limit resolution, which is the standard measure of seeing in adaptive optics.
