# Atmospheric Intensity Scintillation of Stars. I. Statistical Distributions and Temporal Properties

**Dravins et al. (1997)** · *PASP 109, 173* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1997PASP..109..173D) · DOI: [10.1086/133872](https://doi.org/10.1086/133872)

## Summary

First paper of a series on stellar intensity scintillation measured at La Palma with photon-counting detectors and digital signal processors, covering temporal auto- and cross-correlations, power spectra and probability distributions on time scales from microseconds to seasons. Previous studies and the physical mechanisms of scintillation, in which 'flying shadows' are carried by wind and evolve intrinsically, are reviewed. A break in the correlation functions near 300 microseconds may indicate an inner scale of about 3 mm, and the autocorrelation half-width decreases with aperture down to about 5 cm. Near zenith the intensity statistics are best fitted by a beta distribution of the second kind, though log-normal functions are a good approximation.
