# SLODAR: measuring optical turbulence altitude with a Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor

**Wilson (2002)** · *MNRAS 337, 103* · [Free full text](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/337/1/103/1034115) · DOI: [10.1046/j.1365-8711.2002.05847.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-8711.2002.05847.x)

## Summary

Introduces SLODAR (slope detection and ranging), a method for measuring the altitude distribution of optical turbulence with a Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor on a large telescope. The vertical profile is recovered from the time-averaged spatial cross-correlation of the local wavefront slopes measured on the two components of a binary star, by analogy with the SCIDAR technique. The method is tested with Monte Carlo simulations and calibrated against contemporaneous SCIDAR observations. Compared with scintillation-based profiling, the hardware is simpler and more target stars are suitable. A turbulence monitor for the William Herschel Telescope, intended to support AO optimisation and the characterisation of anisoplanatism, is also described.
