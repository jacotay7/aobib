# Focusing on a Turbulent Layer: Principle of the "Generalized SCIDAR"

**Fuchs et al. (1998)** · *PASP 110, 86* · [Free full text](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/316109/pdf) · DOI: [10.1086/316109](https://doi.org/10.1086/316109)

## Summary

Shows that analysing stellar scintillation in a plane conjugated away from the telescope pupil changes which turbulent layers contribute: imaging a layer onto the analysis plane wipes out its scintillation, while layers above or below it are enhanced. Because ground-level scintillation from a layer grows with propagation distance, classical SCIDAR (scintillation detection and ranging) on binary stars is nearly blind to turbulence within about 1 km of the telescope. Moving the analysis plane to a virtual altitude below the ground removes this limitation; this is the "generalized SCIDAR" concept, which allows turbulence profiles to be measured through the whole atmosphere, including the boundary layer. The paper gives the theory, a new interpretation of classical out-of-focus observations, and a laboratory verification using an air vein to simulate a turbulent layer.
