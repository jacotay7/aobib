# Focusing on a Turbulent Layer: Principle of the "Generalized SCIDAR"

**Fuchs et al. (1998)** · *PASP 110, 86* · [Free full text](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/316109/pdf) · DOI: [10.1086/316109](https://doi.org/10.1086/316109)

## Summary

Shows that analysing stellar scintillation in a plane conjugated away from the telescope pupil changes which turbulent layers contribute: imaging a layer onto the analysis plane wipes out its scintillation, while layers above or below it are enhanced. Because the scintillation produced by a layer grows with its distance from the analysis plane, classical SCIDAR (scintillation detection and ranging) on binary stars, which analyses the pupil plane, is nearly blind to turbulence close to the telescope. Moving the analysis plane to a virtual altitude below the ground removes this limitation; this is the "generalized SCIDAR" concept, which allows turbulence profiles to be measured through the whole atmosphere, including the boundary layer. The potential of the concept is shown in a laboratory experiment in which a vein simulates an atmospheric turbulent layer.
