# Using the Cn2 and wind profiler method with wide-field laser-guide-stars adaptive optics to quantify the frozen-flow decay

**Guesalaga et al. (2014)** · *MNRAS 440, 1925* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.6934) · DOI: [10.1093/mnras/stu366](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stu366)

## Summary

Uses spatio-temporal cross-correlations of slopes from the five Shack-Hartmann sensors of GeMS to analyse the temporal evolution of turbulent layers and test the frozen-flow assumption. The Cn2 and wind profiling technique provides the number of layers with their velocities, altitudes and strengths, and a way to estimate the dome-seeing contribution. By tracking identified layers, the rate at which correlation decays in time is estimated from 2011-2013 on-sky data. Results suggest the de-correlation can be described by a single parameter independent of altitude and strength, with a decay rate of the frozen-flow contribution that increases linearly with layer speed, supporting predictive control for wide-field AO.
