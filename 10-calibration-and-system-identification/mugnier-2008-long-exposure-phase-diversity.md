# On-Line Long-Exposure Phase Diversity: a Powerful Tool for Sensing Quasi-Static Aberrations of Extreme Adaptive Optics Imaging Systems

**Mugnier et al. (2008)** · *Optics Express 16, 18406* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.0603) · DOI: [10.1364/OE.16.018406](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.16.018406)

## Summary

Extends the phase-diversity technique to sense quasi-static aberrations, including NCPA, from long-exposure AO-corrected images acquired during science observations. For sufficiently long exposures the residual turbulence averages into a convolutive component of the image, so phase diversity estimates only the static aberrations of interest. Compared with processing short-exposure pairs, the separation of static and turbulent aberrations is done by the exposure itself, only one image pair is processed, and the estimate benefits from the high SNR of long exposures. The method is validated by simulation, and the authors note it can also serve as a phasing sensor for segmented telescopes and for planet finders such as EPICS on the ELT.
