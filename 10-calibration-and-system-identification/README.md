# 10 · Calibration & System Identification

An AO system runs only as well as its model of itself. The interaction matrix links deformable-mirror commands to wavefront-sensor signals. The DM/WFS registration (shift, rotation, magnification) drifts as optics flex and rotate. Static non-common path aberrations (NCPA) between the sensing and science paths are invisible to the loop. The atmospheric and vibration disturbances that optimal controllers and tomographic reconstructors need are statistical quantities. All of these have to be measured, identified, or tracked. On ELT-class telescopes with pre-focal adaptive mirrors (adaptive secondaries, ELT M4), there is often no calibration source upstream of the corrector. This has moved the field from laboratory push-pull measurements towards on-sky, model-based (pseudo-synthetic) and telemetry-based methods.

This folder collects papers on measuring interaction matrices (Hadamard and random-probe schemes, on-sky calibration of adaptive secondaries) and on building and updating synthetic and pseudo-synthetic models, including mis-registration identification and online tracking (SPRINT, GRAVITY+). It also covers NCPA measurement and compensation for general AO with phase diversity and focal-plane methods, and the estimation of turbulence parameters (r0, L0, Cn2 and wind profiles, frozen-flow decay) from AO telemetry. The remaining entries deal with vibration and disturbance identification, data-driven models, and telemetry infrastructure. Pyramid-WFS optical-gain tracking is owned by [02 · Wavefront Sensing](../02-wavefront-sensing/README.md) and appears here through cross-listing. NCPA work that is specific to high-contrast imaging (ZELDA, COFFEE, dark holes) lives in [07](../07-high-contrast-and-extreme-ao/README.md).
