# Experimental verification of dispersed fringe sensing as a segment phasing technique using the Keck telescope

**Shi et al. (2004)** · *Applied Optics 43, 4474* · [Free full text](https://dataverse.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=hdl:2014/40832) · DOI: [10.1364/AO.43.004474](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.43.004474)

## Summary

Dispersed fringe sensing (DFS) is a coarse-phasing method for segmented primary mirrors that covers piston errors from about a quarter wavelength up to the depth of focus of a single segment (tens of microns). Unlike the phasing techniques used on ground-based segmented telescopes, it does not rely on segment edge sensors, which makes it suitable for space telescopes such as JWST. The authors validate DFS on sky by measuring segment piston errors on one of the Keck telescopes. The results agree with the Keck Shack-Hartmann-based phasing scheme to within 2% over initial piston errors of plus or minus 16 µm. The linked copy is in the JPL Open Repository.
