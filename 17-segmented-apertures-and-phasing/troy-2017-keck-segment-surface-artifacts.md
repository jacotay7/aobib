# Keck Segment Surface Artifacts: Impacts on Segment Phasing and Image Quality

**Troy et al. (2017)** · *Proc. AO4ELT5 (Tenerife, 2017)* · [Free full text](https://meetings.iac.es/AO4ELT5/media/proceedings/proceeding-012.pdf) · DOI: [10.26698/AO4ELT5.0012](https://doi.org/10.26698/AO4ELT5.0012)

## Summary

Shows that the Keck primary-mirror segments carry small but significant surface artifacts, most likely by-products of the ion-beam-figuring polishing step, that limit the achievable segment phasing accuracy. The artifacts diffract a significant amount of light beyond ±3.5 arcsec and produce chromatic effects that bias phasing measurements. On-sky measurements of the diffracted intensity and laboratory surface-roughness measurements are presented, the latter showing that roughness is not the cause. The paper also summarizes the context of Keck's broadband and narrowband phasing techniques and discusses implications for segment manufacturing on TMT, which requires phasing to better than 10 nm rms surface.
