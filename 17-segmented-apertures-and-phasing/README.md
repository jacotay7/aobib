# 17 · Segmented Apertures, Phasing & Petaling

Large telescopes increasingly have pupils made of separate pieces: the hexagonal primary-mirror segments of Keck, JWST, TMT and the ELT, the seven 8.4 m mirrors of the GMT, and the pupil "petals" left when wide spider arms cut the aperture. For the telescope to reach its diffraction limit, these pieces must be co-phased to a small fraction of the wavelength. Static and slowly varying segment errors are handled by edge sensors and dedicated phasing sensors. Faster differential pistons between pupil fragments, called petal modes, come from the low-wind effect, from wind buffeting, or from the AO loop itself, and these have to be sensed and corrected during observations. Conventional AO wavefront sensors are often nearly blind to these modes or measure them ambiguously, which is why they have become a key problem for ELT-class AO and for high-contrast imaging.

This folder covers segment phasing concepts and the Keck experience, comparisons of phasing sensors (Shack-Hartmann, curvature, pyramid, Zernike/ZEUS/ZELDA and interferometric sensors), focal-plane and learning-based cophasing, GMT and JWST phasing, the island/petal effect in AO, dedicated petal sensors (petalometers), and the link between segmentation and coronagraph design and tolerancing. General low-wind-effect and focal-plane wavefront control work for high-contrast imaging is in folder 07 and is cross-listed here where it bears on petal modes.

**Subtopics used**

- Segmented-mirror control & Keck phasing: edge sensors, control matrices, the Keck phasing camera and Zernike-sensor segment control at Keck
- Phasing sensors (pupil-plane & interferometric): the Active Phasing Experiment (APE), ZEUS/ZELDA Zernike sensors, pyramid, multi-spectral and interferometric piston sensors
- Focal-plane & learning-based cophasing: dOTF, phase diversity, neural networks
- GMT phasing: the GMT phasing system, holographic dispersed fringe sensor, p-HCAT testbed
- Space-telescope phasing (JWST): commissioning wavefront sensing and control
- Petal modes & island effect in AO: pyramid and Shack-Hartmann behaviour with spiders, mitigation strategies
- Petal sensors (petalometers): sparse-aperture, multi-wavelength and photonic petal sensing
- Segmented-aperture coronagraphy & tolerancing: Lyot coronagraphy with gaps, PASTIS, redundant apodization
