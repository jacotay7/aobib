# Tackling down the low wind effect on SPHERE instrument

**Sauvage et al. (2016)** · *Proc. SPIE 9909, 990916* · [Free full text](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/qjm8c-q3050) · DOI: [10.1117/12.2232459](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2232459)

## Summary

Analyses the low wind effect (LWE), which after more than a year of SPHERE operations at the VLT was found to limit its extreme-AO system SAXO: under low wind, sharp phase discontinuities of up to hundreds of nanometres appear across the telescope spiders, are poorly seen by the AO system, and degrade the PSF. The authors attribute the effect to radiative cooling of the spiders, which cools the air flowing past them, support this scenario with fluid-dynamics simulations, and report that on SPHERE it appears when the wind speed at 30 m drops below about 3 m/s, which happens on roughly 20% of Paranal nights. After comparing ways to measure the LWE, they propose a phase-diversity algorithm whose basis adds 12 piston, tip and tilt modes on the four pupil quadrants; in simulation, correcting the estimated LWE raises the H-band Strehl ratio from 47% to 96%. Introducing an LWE-like pattern through modified wavefront-sensor reference slopes is demonstrated on SPHERE's internal source, while on-sky tests are left for future work, and the low-order vibrations mentioned in the abstract are not treated in the body of the paper.
