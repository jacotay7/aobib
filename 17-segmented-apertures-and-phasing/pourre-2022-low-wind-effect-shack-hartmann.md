# Low-wind-effect impact on Shack-Hartmann-based adaptive optics. Partial control solution in the context of SPHERE and GRAVITY+

**Pourré et al. (2022)** · *A&A 665, A158* · [Free full text](https://insu.hal.science/insu-03860300) · DOI: [10.1051/0004-6361/202243432](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243432)

## Summary

Examines why extreme AO systems with a Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor fail to correct the petal tip and tilt modes created by the low-wind effect, even though these modes leave a measurable signal in the slopes. Simulations of a single subaperture straddling a spider show that, for realistic parameters (spider thickness 25% of a subaperture, field of view 3.5λ/d), the sensitivity to a phase discontinuity is strongly reduced or even reversed. Under the low-wind effect this produces a non-zero curl in the measured slopes and uncontrollable vortex structures in closed-loop residuals. The authors propose controlling petal tip and tilt with the Shack-Hartmann measurements while excluding the faulty subapertures, and evaluate this strategy in simulations relevant to SPHERE and GRAVITY+.
