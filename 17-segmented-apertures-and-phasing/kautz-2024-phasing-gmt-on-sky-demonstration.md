# Phasing the Giant Magellan Telescope: Lab Experiments and First On-sky Demonstration

**Kautz et al. (2024)** · *JATIS 10, 049005* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08388) · DOI: [10.1117/1.JATIS.10.4.049005](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JATIS.10.4.049005)

## Summary

Reviews why pupil fragmentation in giant segmented mirror telescopes, combined with wavefront sensors that have weak or ambiguous sensitivity to differential piston and phase wrapping beyond λ/2, produces petal modes. The holographic dispersed fringe sensor (HDFS) interferes dispersed light from each segment onto separate focal-plane locations, giving a linear piston sensing range of about ±10 µm. Phasing experiments on the GMT High Contrast AO phasing testbed with MagAO-X are reported, together with the first on-sky use of the HDFS as a differential piston sensor. On sky, each segment was phased to within ±λ/11.3 (λ = 800 nm) of a reference segment, with about 50 nm rms residual piston across the aperture in poor seeing.
