# Lessons learned with the Active Phasing Experiment: comparison of four optical phasing sensors on a segmented Very Large Telescope

**Gonté & Surdej (2010)** · *Proc. AO4ELT1 (Paris, 2009), 05005* · [Free full text](https://ao4elt.edpsciences.org/articles/ao4elt/pdf/2010/01/ao4elt_05005.pdf) · DOI: [10.1051/ao4elt/201005005](https://doi.org/10.1051/ao4elt/201005005)

## Summary

Summarizes results of the Active Phasing Experiment (APE), installed at a Nasmyth focus of the VLT Melipal telescope to compare optical phasing sensor concepts for the European ELT. The telescope pupil was segmented into 61 hexagonal segments by an active segmented mirror monitored by a dual-wavelength interferometer, and the beam was shared among four sensors: a pyramid sensor (PYPS), a curvature sensor (DIPSI), a Zernike phase-filtering sensor (ZEUS) and a Shack-Hartmann sensor (SHAPS). On sky, each sensor could phase the mirror, with corrected surfaces typically better than 10 nm RMS for bright stars in seeing better than 1 arcsec, and limiting magnitudes between 14 and 18 depending on the sensor, with PYPS performing best at faint flux. Multi-wavelength techniques extended the capture range to a few microns, and three sensors also corrected segment tip-tilt to better than 0.4 arcsec rms. The authors note that behaviour under AO-corrected conditions remained to be studied.
