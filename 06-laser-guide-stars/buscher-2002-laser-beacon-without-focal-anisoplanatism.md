# Laser beacon wave-front sensing without focal anisoplanatism

**Buscher et al. (2002)** · *Optics Letters 27, 149* · [Free full text](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1591129) · DOI: [10.1364/OL.27.000149](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.27.000149)

## Summary

Conventional laser-beacon wavefront sensing forms a focused spot in the atmosphere and measures the distortions accumulated on the return path, which leads to focal anisoplanatism (the cone effect). The letter proposes instead to sense the distortions imposed on the outgoing beam by projecting a collimated intensity pattern into the atmosphere and imaging it from the ground. Because a parallel beam is used, the measurements do not suffer from focal anisoplanatism, and the authors outline several geometries based on projecting a pupil pattern onto the atmosphere. The concept was later developed at Durham as the projected pupil plane pattern (PPPP) technique.
