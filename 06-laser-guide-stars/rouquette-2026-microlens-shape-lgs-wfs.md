# Impact of microlens shape on the performance of Laser Guide Star wavefront sensors for ELT-class telescopes

**Rouquette et al. (2026)** · *Proc. SPIE 14150 (Adaptive Optics Systems X)* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26543) · DOI: [10.1117/12.3102513](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3102513)

## Summary

Quantifies how manufacturing imperfections in the surface profile of Shack-Hartmann microlenses affect laser guide star wavefront sensing on ELT-class telescopes, using the MORFEO-HARMONI LGS wavefront sensor design as the main test case. Starting from interferometric surface measurements of prototype microlenses, the authors derive the induced phase errors and compute the degradation of center-of-gravity centroiding accuracy as a function of LGS spot elongation and flux. A real microlens increases the centroid variance by a factor of 1.8 to 2.4 relative to an ideal lens, roughly equivalent to needing twice the photon flux; the penalty decreases with elongation and improves with higher microlens sag. Propagated into end-to-end tomographic AO simulations, measurement redundancy across several LGS and MMSE weighting reduce the effective flux-loss factor to 1.25 to 1.4. The results are validated on the LAM LGS wavefront sensor bench.
