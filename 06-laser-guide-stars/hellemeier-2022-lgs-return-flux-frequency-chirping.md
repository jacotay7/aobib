# Laser guide star return-flux gain from frequency chirping

**Hellemeier et al. (2022)** · *MNRAS 511, 4660* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00252) · DOI: [10.1093/mnras/stac343](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac343)

## Summary

Photon recoil Doppler-shifts sodium atoms out of resonance with a single-frequency laser (spectral hole burning), reducing guide-star return flux; frequency-chirped continuous-wave lasers could mitigate this and exceed the single-frequency limit, as predicted by earlier Bloch-equation studies. The paper reports on-sky measurements of a frequency-chirped single-frequency LGS at the Roque de los Muchachos Observatory on La Palma, using a 35 cm telescope and a fast photon-counting receiver to resolve the return-flux response during frequency sweeps. At 16.5 W launched power, a maximum return-flux gain of 22% over a fixed-frequency laser is found, at a chirp amplitude of about 150 MHz and a rate of 0.8 MHz per microsecond, consistent with theory. The gain depends strongly on mesospheric power density, so larger gains are expected for higher-power lasers.
