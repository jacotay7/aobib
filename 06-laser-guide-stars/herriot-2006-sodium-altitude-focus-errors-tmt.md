# Focus errors from tracking sodium layer altitude variations with laser guide star adaptive optics for the Thirty Meter Telescope

**Herriot et al. (2006)** · *Proc. SPIE 6272, 62721I* · [Free full text](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/6xm0b-75p48) · DOI: [10.1117/12.672081](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.672081)

## Summary

Wavefront errors from mis-focusing LGS wavefront sensors on the mesospheric sodium layer scale with the square of the telescope diameter and are therefore important for extremely large telescopes: on a 30 m telescope, 15 m of sodium-altitude defocus causes about 120 nm of residual error. Because sodium-altitude focus changes overlap in temporal frequency with atmospheric focus, only natural-guide-star sensors can separate them, and the paper analyses the resulting focus-tracking errors for TMT when corrections are applied either with modest-bandwidth focus mechanisms or as offsets from photon-starved NGS measurements. The analysis relies on extrapolating sodium-profile power spectra measured at 1-2 minute intervals to frequencies above 1 Hz. The authors note that a pulsed laser with dynamic refocusing on a polar-coordinate CCD could in principle eliminate this focus-tracking error, in addition to mitigating spot elongation.
