# Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensing with elongated sodium laser beacons: centroiding versus matched filtering

**Gilles & Ellerbroek (2006)** · *Applied Optics 45, 6568* · [Free full text](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/h8raa-vzm42) · DOI: [10.1364/AO.45.006568](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.45.006568)

## Summary

The paper presents modelling and simulation results for the Thirty Meter Telescope on the degradation of sodium LGS Shack-Hartmann measurement accuracy caused by the spatial structure and temporal variability of the mesospheric sodium layer. Using a contiguous set of sodium lidar profiles, it compares a standard centroid with a noise-optimal matched-filter spot-position estimator for elongated spots, at a nominal signal of 1000 photodetected electrons per subaperture per frame, as a function of the subaperture distance from the laser launch telescope and of CCD read noise. The two estimators are compared in terms of noise-induced spot-position error, the resulting wavefront error for the TMT facility AO system, linear dynamic range, and the radial bias that appears when the estimator is not updated quickly enough to track changes in the sodium profile. The work underlies the matched-filter approach (with radial-format CCDs) considered for TMT LGS wavefront sensing.
