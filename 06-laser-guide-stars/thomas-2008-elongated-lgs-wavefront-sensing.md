# Study of optimal wavefront sensing with elongated laser guide stars

**Thomas et al. (2008)** · *MNRAS 387, 173* · [Free full text](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/387/1/173/1013233) · DOI: [10.1111/j.1365-2966.2008.13110.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2008.13110.x)

## Summary

Sodium LGS spots are elongated in Shack-Hartmann subapertures far from the launch axis, which lowers signal-to-noise and increases wavefront measurement error. The paper proposes a polar-coordinate CCD in which the size, shape and orientation of each subaperture pixel array are adapted to the local spot elongation, reducing the number of pixels read and allowing faster frame rates for ELTs. Centre-of-gravity, weighted centre-of-gravity and correlation centroiders are compared on this geometry in terms of centroid error variance and linearity. Correlation tracking is found to give slightly better noise performance, and an optimum sampling of about 1.5 pixels per FWHM is identified (e.g. 4 x 16 pixel arrays for an elongation ratio of 4); the effects of sodium-layer variability are left to future work.
