# ELT-scale elongated LGS wavefront sensing: on-sky results

**Bardou et al. (2021)** · *A&A 649, A158* · [Free full text](https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202038330/pdf) · DOI: [10.1051/0004-6361/202038330](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202038330)

## Summary

The paper studies on sky the impact of ELT-scale laser guide star elongation on wavefront sensing, using the CANARY demonstrator on the William Herschel Telescope observing a natural guide star with a superimposed LGS launched by the ESO Wendelstein unit 40 m from the pupil. Comparing wavefronts from the two guide stars gives an error breakdown of elongated-LGS wavefront sensing. The elongated-LGS sensor was successfully used to drive the AO loop but required regular calibration of non-common path aberrations; the elongation-related error splits into a dynamic part (0-160 nm rms, linked to turbulence strength and elongation) and a quasi-static part (20-200 nm rms). Undersampling with pixels up to 1.95 arcsec had no visible impact, but truncation produced significant errors for subaperture fields smaller than 10.4-15.6 arcsec, implying a field of about 16.3 arcsec for the ELT at zenith if the most elongated spots are placed along the subaperture diagonal.
