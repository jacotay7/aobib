# 06 · Laser Guide Stars

Laser guide stars (LGS) are artificial reference sources created by projecting a laser into the atmosphere, either by Rayleigh scattering from air molecules at roughly 10–25 km or by resonant fluorescence of the mesospheric sodium layer at about 90 km. They free adaptive optics from the need for a bright natural star next to the science target, but bring their own problems: the finite altitude of the beacon causes the cone effect (focal anisoplanatism), the atmospheric tip-tilt cannot be measured from the laser spot, the sodium layer is thick and variable so spots appear elongated and the focus drifts, and the lasers themselves are complex, expensive and subject to safety and operational constraints.

This folder collects papers on the physics and engineering of LGS: the original concept papers, sodium-atom physics and return-flux optimization (optical pumping, polarization, Larmor precession, recoil, repumping, chirping), the structure and variability of the mesospheric sodium layer, Rayleigh beacons, guide-star lasers and launch facilities, the cone effect, tip-tilt indetermination and its proposed solutions, LGS wavefront sensing with elongated spots (truncation, matched filters, detectors, ELT designs), on-sky LGS AO systems, and operational side effects such as Raman contamination and laser traffic control. Multi-LGS tomography itself lives in [05](../05-wide-field-and-tomographic-ao/README.md); facility AO systems that happen to use lasers are mostly in [12](../12-ao-systems-and-instruments/README.md) and are cross-listed here.
