# Characterization of the sodium layer at Cerro Pachón, and impact on laser guide star performance

**Neichel et al. (2013)** · *MNRAS 429, 3522* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3690) · DOI: [10.1093/mnras/sts631](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/sts631)

## Summary

The paper analyses two data sets on the mesospheric sodium layer above Cerro Pachón/Cerro Tololo in Chile: a 2001 laser experiment monitoring sodium abundance and altitude over six runs in one year, and LGS wavefront-sensor data from the first year of commissioning and operation of the Gemini MCAO system (GeMS) with five laser guide stars. From these, the authors derive sodium column density, layer thickness, mean-altitude behaviour, photon return and spot elongation. All parameters show large variations on yearly, nightly and hourly timescales: photon return changes by a factor of three to four over a year and by a factor of two within a night. The temporal power spectrum of the sodium mean altitude follows a power law consistent with Pfrommer and Hickson (2010), and a comparison with the 2001 data shows a large difference in laser-format efficiency.
