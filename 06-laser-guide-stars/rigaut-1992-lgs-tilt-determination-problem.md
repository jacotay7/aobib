# Laser guide star in adaptive optics: the tilt determination problem

**Rigaut & Gendron (1992)** · *A&A 261, 677* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1992A%26A...261..677R)

## Summary

The paper shows that a laser guide star cannot provide the global wavefront tilt, because the upward and downward paths of the laser light suffer the same atmospheric tip-tilt, so a natural reference star is still needed for tilt sensing. Taking photon noise and anisoplanatic errors into account, the authors compare the performance of adaptive optics using natural and laser reference stars and compute sky coverage for both cases. They find that sky coverage remains low for correction at visible wavelengths. As a mitigation they propose a dual adaptive optics concept in which the tilt reference star is itself corrected using the laser beacon, which sharpens it, improves tilt-sensing accuracy, and is predicted to extend sky coverage toward visible wavelengths on large telescopes.
