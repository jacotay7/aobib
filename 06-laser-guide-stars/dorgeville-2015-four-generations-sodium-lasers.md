# From Dye Laser Factory to Portable Semiconductor Laser: Four Generations of Sodium Guide Star Lasers for Adaptive Optics in Astronomy and Space Situational Awareness

**d'Orgeville & Fetzer (2015)** · *AMOS Technical Conference 2015* · [Free full text](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2015/Adaptive_Optics_Imaging/dOrgeville.pdf) · DOI: [10.64861/ltkm2905](https://doi.org/10.64861/ltkm2905)

## Summary

The paper reviews the history of sodium guide-star lasers used in astronomical and space-situational-awareness adaptive optics and the impact of laser technology on routine telescope operations, grouping systems into technology generations. First-generation dye lasers created the first sodium guide stars in Hawaii, California and Spain in the late 1980s and 1990s and equipped the first LGS facilities, but were large, power-hungry and messy. A second generation of solid-state lasers developed in the USA in the 2000s equipped 8-10 m telescopes and 4 m-class satellite-imaging facilities, while a third generation based on compact fibre lasers was developed in Europe and resulted in the only commercially available sodium guide-star laser system at the time. The authors propose a fourth generation based on semiconductor lasers. A related paper by the same authors appeared in Proc. SPIE 9909 (2016).
