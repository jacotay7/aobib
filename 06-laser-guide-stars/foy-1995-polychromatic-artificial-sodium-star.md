# The polychromatic artificial sodium star: a new concept for correcting the atmospheric tilt

**Foy et al. (1995)** · *A&AS 111, 569* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1995A%26AS..111..569F)

## Summary

The paper introduces the polychromatic laser guide star, a concept to measure wavefront tilt without a natural guide star. Because the refractive index of air varies with wavelength, the tilt can be inferred from differential tilt measurements between images of the artificial star at different wavelengths. The authors propose creating such a source in mesospheric sodium by resonant two-photon excitation with lasers at 589 and 569 nm, which produces a radiative cascade from 0.33 to 2.3 microns; they model the population of the 4D level with rate equations. They derive the return flux required to measure the differential tilt as a function of Fried parameter and wavelength, find it to be independent of telescope diameter for D/r0 > 1, and estimate the corresponding laser power and possible ways of reducing it.
