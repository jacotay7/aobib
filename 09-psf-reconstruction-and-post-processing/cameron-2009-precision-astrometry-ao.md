# Precision Astrometry with Adaptive Optics

**Cameron et al. (2009)** · *AJ 137, 83* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2153) · DOI: [10.1088/0004-6256/137/1/83](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/137/1/83)

## Summary

The paper investigates the limits of ground-based differential astrometry with AO using the core of the globular cluster M5 observed at the Palomar Hale telescope. The dominant systematic errors are described along with observational strategies to mitigate them; after these measures, tilt anisoplanatism is the dominant limitation. The authors present an optimal estimation technique for the position of a star relative to a grid of reference stars that accounts for this correlated noise, with errors decreasing as the square root of time and faster than the square root of the number of references. With 50 reference stars they report about 1 mas precision in 1 s and below 100 microarcseconds in 2 minutes, and accuracy below about 100 microarcseconds over two months.
