# Characterizing specimen induced aberrations for high NA adaptive optical microscopy

**Schwertner et al. (2004)** · *Optics Express 12, 6540* · [Free full text](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPEX.12.006540) · DOI: [10.1364/OPEX.12.006540](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPEX.12.006540)

## Summary

Quantifies the aberrations introduced by biological specimens in order to inform the design of adaptive optics microscopes. The authors built an interferometer with high-NA objective lenses to measure specimen-induced wavefronts and decomposed them into Zernike modes. From these data they calculated the potential signal benefit of correcting different numbers of modes at different NAs in an adaptive confocal fluorescence microscope. The results indicate that correcting low-order Zernike modes can provide substantial benefit for many specimens, and that quantitative fluorescence microscopy can be strongly affected by uncorrected specimen aberrations.
