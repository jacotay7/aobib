# Characterization and adaptive optical correction of aberrations during in vivo imaging in the mouse cortex

**Ji et al. (2012)** · *PNAS 109, 22* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3252919/) · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1109202108](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1109202108)

## Summary

Characterises the optical aberrations encountered during in vivo two-photon imaging of the mouse brain and corrects them with pupil-segmentation adaptive optics. The authors find that aberrations vary across the sample and grow with depth but remain stable for hours, so a correction can be reused. Adaptive correction recovered diffraction-limited performance to depths of 450 µm and improved image quality over fields of view of hundreds of microns. Reported gains include a fivefold signal enhancement for small neuronal structures, a threefold increase in axial resolution, and improved signal-to-noise ratio in functional calcium imaging of single neurons.
