# Superpenetration optical microscopy by iterative multiphoton adaptive compensation technique

**Tang et al. (2012)** · *PNAS 109, 8434* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3365222/) · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1119590109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1119590109)

## Summary

Introduces the iterative multiphoton adaptive compensation technique (IMPACT), which uses the nonlinearity of the multiphoton signal to iteratively measure and compensate wavefront distortions in deep tissue. Unlike conventional adaptive optics, the method handles highly complex wavefronts and compensates not only aberrations but also random scattering, focusing light inside deep tissue. It is tested on heterogeneous samples including mouse brain tissue, skull and lymph nodes. The authors show three-dimensional imaging beyond the depth reached by conventional multiphoton microscopy and adaptive optics, over limited regions per correction, with reduced excitation power requirements.
