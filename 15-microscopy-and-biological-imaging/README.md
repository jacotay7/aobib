# 15 · AO for Microscopy & Biological Imaging

Adaptive optics in microscopy corrects aberrations that arise mainly inside the
specimen: refractive-index mismatch at the coverslip and immersion medium, and
the inhomogeneous refractive-index structure of cells and tissue. These
aberrations reduce signal, contrast and resolution, and get worse with depth.
Compared with astronomical AO, microscope AO usually has no natural point
source. The aberration is often stable over seconds to hours but varies
strongly across the field, and the photon budget is limited by photobleaching
and phototoxicity. For these reasons the field developed its own
techniques. These include wavefront-sensorless (image-based) modal correction
and zonal pupil-segmentation methods. Direct Shack-Hartmann sensing uses
fluorescent, nonlinear or near-infrared "guide stars" in tissue, and
coherence-gated and interferometric focus-sensing methods are also used.
Conjugate and multi-pupil schemes extend the corrected field of view.

This folder collects reviews and key method and application papers for these
techniques. It covers confocal, multiphoton (two- and three-photon) and in
vivo neuroimaging, light-sheet and lattice light-sheet microscopy, and
super-resolution methods (STED, single-molecule localization, structured
illumination). It also includes open software, build protocols and
learning-based or computational aberration correction. Each entry links to a
free full text, either open access or an author manuscript in PubMed Central,
HAL or another repository. Important paywalled papers are listed separately in
the registry's paywalled list.
