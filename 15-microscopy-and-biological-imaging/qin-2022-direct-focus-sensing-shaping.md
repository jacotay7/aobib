# Deep tissue multi-photon imaging using adaptive optics with direct focus sensing and shaping

**Qin et al. (2022)** · *Nature Biotechnology 40, 1663* · [Free full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.08.04.455159v1) · DOI: [10.1038/s41587-022-01343-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01343-w)

## Summary

Presents an adaptive optics three-photon microscope based on analog lock-in phase detection for focus sensing and shaping (ALPHA-FSS; called direct focus sensing and shaping, DFSS, in the preprint), which measures and compensates both aberrations and scattering induced by the specimen and recovers subcellular resolution at depth. A weak scanning beam interferes with a strong stationary beam, and phase modulation with lock-in detection recovers the aberrated electric-field PSF in the tissue; the correction is obtained by phase conjugation and applied with a high-pixel-count spatial light modulator in a conjugate AO configuration combined with remote focusing. The system images fine neuronal structures in the mouse cortex through the intact skull down to 750 µm below the pia, and is used for functional calcium imaging and laser microsurgery through the skull. Imaging of deep cortex and subcortical hippocampus up to 1.1 mm below the pia in the intact brain is also reported. The free link is the bioRxiv preprint (titled "Direct focus sensing and shaping for high-resolution multi-photon imaging in deep tissue").
