# Pupil-segmentation-based adaptive optical microscopy with full-pupil illumination

**Milkie, Betzig & Ji (2011)** · *Optics Letters 36, 4206* · [Free full text](https://www.janelia.org/sites/default/files/Library/Optics%20Letters%202011%2036%20%2821%29%204206.pdf) · DOI: [10.1364/OL.36.004206](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.36.004206)

## Summary

This letter presents a variant of pupil-segmentation adaptive optics in which the whole pupil stays illuminated during the aberration measurement, instead of one segment at a time. With the wavefront fixed on all other segments, a series of phase ramps applied to one segment scans its beamlet across the aberrated focus; interference with the remaining light modulates the fluorescence signal, and the shift of the resulting intensity extremum gives the local wavefront tilt of that segment. The procedure is iterated as the reference focus improves. Implemented in a two-photon fluorescence microscope, it recovers diffraction-limited performance and improves signal and resolution. Because the full numerical aperture is used, the measurement needs less excitation power and has a shorter depth of focus, which makes it usable in dense three-dimensional samples where single-segment illumination gives erroneous corrections.
