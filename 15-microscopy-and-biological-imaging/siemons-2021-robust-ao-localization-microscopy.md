# Robust adaptive optics for localization microscopy deep in complex tissue

**Siemons et al. (2021)** · *Nature Communications 12, 3407* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8184833/) · DOI: [10.1038/s41467-021-23647-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23647-2)

## Summary

Systematically compares existing adaptive optics approaches for single-molecule localization microscopy (SMLM) using simulations and experiments with standardised samples, and finds that they often give limited correction or even introduce errors. Analysis of the causes led to an improved method, REALM (Robust and Effective Adaptive Optics in Localization Microscopy), which corrects aberrations up to 1 rad rms using 297 frames of blinking molecules. After quantitative validation, REALM is used to resolve the periodic organisation of spectrin in the axon initial segment at 50 µm depth in brain tissue.
