# Adaptive optics via pupil segmentation for high-resolution imaging in biological tissues

**Ji, Milkie & Betzig (2010)** · *Nature Methods 7, 141* · [Free full text](https://www.janelia.org/sites/default/files/Library/Nature%20Methods%202009%20Ji%20AO%20Two%20Photon%20with%20Supplementary%20Figures.pdf) · DOI: [10.1038/nmeth.1411](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.1411)

## Summary

This paper introduced pupil-segmentation adaptive optics for microscopy. The rear pupil of the objective is divided into subregions using a spatial light modulator, and light is directed through one subregion at a time; the image shift measured for each subregion gives the local wavefront tilt experienced by that group of rays as they travel through the specimen. The phase of each segment is then obtained either by direct interference measurements or by phase reconstruction, and the corrective pattern is applied with the same modulator. Applied to two-photon microscopy, the method recovered near-diffraction-limited performance in biological and non-biological samples with large or small, smooth or abruptly varying aberrations, and improved signal and resolution in fixed mouse cortical slices to depths of 400 µm.
