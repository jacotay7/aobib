# Contribution of statistical site learning to improve optical turbulence forecasting

**Giordano et al. (2021)** · *MNRAS 504, 1927* · [Free full text](https://hal.science/hal-03266390) · DOI: [10.1093/mnras/staa3709](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staa3709)

## Summary

Describes 'site learning', in which local turbulence measurements are used to statistically correct a numerical optical-turbulence forecasting model so that it accounts for the specificities of a given site. The study uses the database of the Calern Atmospheric Turbulence Station (operational since 2015 at Calern Observatory) and several months of model predictions, considering daytime and night-time conditions. The authors report that site learning reduces the absolute bias between measurements and predictions by 25 to 50 per cent for each Cn2 layer, by 25 per cent for seeing and by 70 per cent for the isoplanatic angle. Applications include flexible scheduling of observations and long-term site characterization.
