# High-Dynamic-Range Imaging Using a Deformable Mirror for Space Coronography

**Malbet et al. (1995)** · *PASP 107, 386* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9502042) · DOI: [10.1086/133563](https://doi.org/10.1086/133563)

## Summary

Proposes using a deformable mirror to correct optical-surface imperfections and reduce scattered light in selected regions of a coronagraphic image, introducing the "dark hole" concept. The authors show that a dark-hole algorithm yields lower scattering than minimizing RMS figure error, and that the achievable level depends on actuator count, telescope surface quality, influence function and wavelength. Simulations with a 37x37 deformable mirror and Hubble Space Telescope optics demonstrate rectangular and annular dark holes, suggesting dynamic ranges beyond 1e8. A concept for a monolithic high-density deformable mirror is also presented.
