# Extreme Adaptive Optics

**Guyon (2018)** · *ARA&A 56, 315* · [Free full text](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-astro-081817-052000) · DOI: [10.1146/annurev-astro-081817-052000](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-astro-081817-052000)

## Summary

A review of extreme adaptive optics (ExAO): AO systems optimized to deliver very high contrast close to bright stars for direct imaging of exoplanets and circumstellar disks. It frames the observational challenge (planets typically within about 1 arcsec of their host star and 4 to 10 orders of magnitude fainter) and discusses the wavefront sensing, wavefront control, coronagraphy and data-analysis techniques that set the achievable contrast. Current ExAO systems are described as able to image thermal emission from young giant planets in the near-infrared. The review then looks ahead to systems on giant segmented-mirror telescopes, which the author expects to reach reflected-light imaging and spectroscopy of lower-mass and potentially habitable rocky planets.
