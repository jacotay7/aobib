# Limits of Adaptive Optics for High-Contrast Imaging

**Guyon (2005)** · *ApJ 629, 592* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0505086) · DOI: [10.1086/431209](https://doi.org/10.1086/431209)

## Summary

Evaluates how photon noise, aliasing, wavefront chromaticity and scintillation limit the point-spread-function contrast achievable with ground-based adaptive optics, for several wavefront-sensing schemes. The analysis finds that a Zernike phase-contrast wavefront sensor has the best photon-noise sensitivity at all spatial frequencies, while the Shack-Hartmann sensor is markedly less sensitive. When sensing in the visible and imaging in the near-infrared, scintillation chromaticity from Fresnel propagation limits contrast to about 1e-4 to 1e-5 within the central arcsecond of an 8 m telescope. Sensing and imaging at the same wavelength would allow 1e-6 to 1e-7 on bright stars, and the paper quantifies the dependence on seeing, wind speed and turbulence profile and argues for focal-plane wavefront sensing.
