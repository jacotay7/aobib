# Angular Differential Imaging: A Powerful High-Contrast Imaging Technique

**Marois et al. (2006)** · *ApJ 641, 556* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0512335) · DOI: [10.1086/500401](https://doi.org/10.1086/500401)

## Summary

Introduces angular differential imaging (ADI), in which an altitude/azimuth telescope observes with the field derotator off so that the instrument and telescope optics stay aligned while the sky rotates. For each frame, a reference PSF built from other frames of the sequence is subtracted to remove quasi-static structure, and the residuals are derotated and combined. On Gemini North data each subtraction reduces quasi-static PSF noise by a factor of about 5, and one-hour sequences give total speckle attenuation of 20-50 relative to a single 30 s exposure.
