# Speckle lifetimes in high-contrast adaptive optics

**Macintosh et al. (2005)** · *Proc. SPIE 5903, 59030J* · [Free full text](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/875947) · DOI: [10.1117/12.627854](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.627854)

## Summary

Studies the lifetime of the atmospheric speckles left by an adaptive optics system, which set a basic noise floor for detecting faint companions near bright stars because the delay between sensing and correction always leaves a residual speckle pattern. Using a systematic series of simulations, the authors measure how quickly the speckle pattern averages to a smooth halo. They distinguish first-order speckles, where diffraction matters and speckles are modulated quickly as a phase error moves across the aperture, from second-order speckles in the coronagraphic regime, which only evolve once the phase screen has fully crossed the aperture. The second-order speckle lifetime is found to be nearly independent of the AO system properties and set mainly by the clearing time of the telescope aperture. Quasi-static speckles from calibration errors and optics are explicitly left out of scope. The free copy is the LLNL report UCRL-CONF-214737 on OSTI.
