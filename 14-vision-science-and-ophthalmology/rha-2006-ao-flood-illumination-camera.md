# Adaptive optics flood-illumination camera for high speed retinal imaging

**Rha et al. (2006)** · *Optics Express 14, 4552* · [Free full text](https://opg.optica.org/oe/fulltext.cfm?uri=oe-14-10-4552) · DOI: [10.1364/OE.14.004552](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.14.004552)

## Summary

Describes a bench-top AO flood-illumination retina camera that reaches frame rates well above the sub-7 Hz typical of earlier systems, using strobed fibre-coupled superluminescent and laser diodes with a scientific CCD. Continuous rates of 10, 30 and 60 Hz were obtained for 1.8, 0.8 and 0.4 degree fields, and short bursts up to 500 Hz by on-chip storage. Short exposures (about 1 ms), high frame rates and aberration correction were found necessary to individuate blood cells and measure capillary flow. Videos of dark-adapted eyes showed rapid (~1 Hz) reflectance fluctuations of single cones, and the trade-off between exposure duration and motion blur was quantified.
