# Imaging and quantifying ganglion cells and other transparent neurons in the living human retina

**Liu et al. (2017)** · *PNAS 114, 12803* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5715765/) · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1711734114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711734114)

## Summary

Shows that singly scattered light, captured with AO-OCT, carries enough information to image ganglion cell somas, axons and other transparent retinal neurons in the living human eye. The approach combines AO-OCT to remove ocular blur, 3D subcellular registration to remove motion blur, and the use of organelle motility within somas as an intrinsic contrast agent through temporal averaging. The authors perform morphometry of ganglion-cell-layer somas, including projection onto photoreceptors and identification of primary ganglion cell subtypes beneath the nerve fibre layer. Through-focus imaging is proposed for mapping ganglion cells to underlying retinal cell layers.
