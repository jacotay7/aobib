# High-speed adaptive optics line-scan OCT for cellular-resolution optoretinography

**Pandiyan et al. (2020)** · *Biomedical Optics Express 11, 5274* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7510866/) · DOI: [10.1364/BOE.399034](https://doi.org/10.1364/BOE.399034)

## Summary

Introduces a line-scan spectral-domain OCT with adaptive optics for volumetric imaging and phase-resolved recording of retinal responses to light. Acquiring an entire B-scan in a single camera frame gave depth-resolved tomograms at up to 16 kHz with high sensitivity and phase stability despite eye motion. An anamorphic detection scheme improved light collection and signal roll-off relative to conventional designs. Nanometre-scale, millisecond light-induced optical path length changes were recorded in cone outer segments, and responses to 660 nm stimuli separated individual cones into L, M and S clusters.
