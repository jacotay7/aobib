# In vivo autofluorescence imaging of the human and macaque retinal pigment epithelial cell mosaic

**Morgan et al. (2009)** · *IOVS 50, 1350* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2790524/) · DOI: [10.1167/iovs.08-2618](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.08-2618)

## Summary

Extends fluorescence AOSLO imaging of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) from anaesthetized macaques to fixating human subjects. One channel recorded cone reflectance while a second detected lipofuscin autofluorescence (568 nm excitation, emission around 624 nm); motion measured in the reflectance channel was used to register and average the autofluorescence frames. RPE cell density and mosaic regularity decreased with eccentricity, while cell size and spacing increased. Repeat imaging 42 days apart showed the same cells, and the authors present the method as a basis for longitudinal studies of RPE disease.
