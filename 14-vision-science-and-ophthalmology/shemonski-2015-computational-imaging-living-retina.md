# Computational high-resolution optical imaging of the living human retina

**Shemonski et al. (2015)** · *Nat. Photonics 9, 440* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4750047/) · DOI: [10.1038/nphoton.2015.102](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphoton.2015.102)

## Summary

Demonstrates a fully automated computational approach to high-resolution in vivo retinal imaging that replaces hardware adaptive optics. The method applies computational aberration correction to phase-stable en face OCT data of the living human eye, after correcting motion-induced phase errors. Cone photoreceptors were resolved without a deformable mirror or wavefront sensor. The authors argue that this reduces the complexity and cost associated with hardware AO and shows that computational methods from coherent microscopy apply in dynamic living systems.
