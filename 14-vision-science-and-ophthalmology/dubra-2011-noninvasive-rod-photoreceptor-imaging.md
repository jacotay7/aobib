# Noninvasive imaging of the human rod photoreceptor mosaic using a confocal adaptive optics scanning ophthalmoscope

**Dubra et al. (2011)** · *Biomedical Optics Express 2, 1864* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130574/) · DOI: [10.1364/BOE.2.001864](https://doi.org/10.1364/BOE.2.001864)

## Summary

Reports in vivo images of the contiguous human rod photoreceptor mosaic in nine healthy subjects, acquired with three confocal AO scanning ophthalmoscopes at two institutions using 680 and 775 nm illumination. Photoreceptor densities and rod:cone ratios between 5 and 15 degrees eccentricity agreed with histology, obtained by averaging registered frames without further processing. In one subject the first rods were identified about 190 micrometres from the foveal centre. Rod and cone mosaics came into best focus at different depths, the rods at least 10 micrometres shallower beyond 8 degrees.
