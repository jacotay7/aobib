# Adaptive optics scanning laser ophthalmoscope using a micro-electro-mechanical (MEMS) deformable mirror

**Zhang et al. (2006)** · *Proc. SPIE 6138 (Ophthalmic Technologies XVI), 61380Z* · [Free full text](http://roorda.vision.berkeley.edu/Pubs/ZhangPoonjaRoordaSPIE6138.pdf) · DOI: [10.1117/12.646934](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.646934)

## Summary

Describes a new-generation adaptive optics scanning laser ophthalmoscope (AOSLO) built around a Boston Micromachines MEMS deformable mirror for in vivo imaging of the human retina. The small aperture of the MEMS mirror allowed a compact, robust system on a mobile 30 by 30 inch breadboard while keeping system aberrations diffraction-limited over fields up to 3 by 3 degrees. A customized Shack-Hartmann sensor drives modal correction of ocular aberrations over a 6 mm pupil, applied on both the ingoing and outgoing paths. After correction the rms wave aberration was below 0.1 μm for most eyes, cones were resolved near the foveal center, and the higher throughput allowed a confocal pinhole smaller than the Airy disc. The companion journal paper (Zhang, Poonja & Roorda 2006, Optics Letters) is not free; this proceedings paper is on the authors' lab site.
