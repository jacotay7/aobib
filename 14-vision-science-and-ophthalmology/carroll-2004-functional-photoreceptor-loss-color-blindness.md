# Functional photoreceptor loss revealed with adaptive optics: an alternate cause of color blindness

**Carroll et al. (2004)** · *PNAS 101, 8461* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC420416/) · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.0401440101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0401440101)

## Summary

Uses adaptive optics retinal imaging, together with photopigment gene analysis, to examine the cone mosaics of red-green dichromats. In a subject whose M-pigment gene encodes a nonfunctional pigment, the mosaic of L and S cones appeared normal but contained dark patches where M cones would have been, indicating loss of the entire corresponding cone class. This contradicted the earlier view that common inherited colour blindness is not caused by photoreceptor loss. Despite losing about one third of the cones, the subject showed no visual impairment other than in colour vision.
