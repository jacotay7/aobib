# 14 · AO for Vision Science & Ophthalmology

Adaptive optics was brought to the eye in the late 1990s, when Hartmann-Shack aberrometry and a deformable mirror were combined to correct the eye's higher-order aberrations, improving both vision and the resolution of retinal images. The eye is an unusual AO target: the "atmosphere" is the cornea and crystalline lens, aberrations are large and change slowly compared with astronomical turbulence, the guide star is a beacon focused on the retina, and light exposure is limited by safety standards. Since then, AO has been built into flood-illumination fundus cameras, confocal and non-confocal scanning laser ophthalmoscopes (AOSLO), and optical coherence tomography (AO-OCT). Together these resolve individual photoreceptors, retinal pigment epithelial cells, ganglion cells and blood cells in the living eye. AO is also used to control the optics of the eye in psychophysical experiments, for example to test visual corrections in visual simulators or to stimulate single cones.

This folder collects free-to-read papers on these topics: measurement and statistics of ocular aberrations, ocular AO hardware, the main imaging modalities, wavefront-sensorless and computational aberration correction for the eye, functional imaging (optoretinography), AO psychophysics, and clinical and cell-level applications. AO in microscopy and general tissue imaging is covered in [15](../15-microscopy-and-biological-imaging/README.md). Deformable-mirror technology in general is covered in [03](../03-deformable-mirrors-and-correctors/README.md).
