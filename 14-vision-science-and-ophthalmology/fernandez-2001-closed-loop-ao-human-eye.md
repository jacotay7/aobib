# Closed-loop adaptive optics in the human eye

**Fernández et al. (2001)** · *Optics Letters 26, 746* · [Free full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5808258_Closed-loop_adaptive_optics_in_the_human_eye) · DOI: [10.1364/OL.26.000746](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.26.000746)

## Summary

Describes a prototype for real-time closed-loop measurement and correction of the aberrations of the human eye. Aberrations are measured in infrared light at 25 Hz with a Hartmann-Shack sensor, defocus is removed with a motorized optometer, and higher-order aberrations are corrected with a membrane deformable mirror. Static aberrations are corrected in about five iterations, so the system can follow changes at about 5 Hz. In living eyes the residual wavefront error was about 0.1 μm over a 4.3 mm pupil, and retinal images of a point source were estimated in real time with and without correction. The authors suggest the device could act as electro-optic 'spectacles' to improve vision.
