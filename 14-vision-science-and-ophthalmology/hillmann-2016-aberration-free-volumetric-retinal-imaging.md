# Aberration-free volumetric high-speed imaging of in vivo retina

**Hillmann et al. (2016)** · *Scientific Reports 6, 35209* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5071870/) · DOI: [10.1038/srep35209](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep35209)

## Summary

Presents a parallelized (full-field, swept-source) OCT setup acquiring about 10 billion voxels per second, an order of magnitude faster than previous retinal OCT. The phase-stable volumetric data allow defocus and ocular aberrations to be determined and corrected computationally after acquisition, giving diffraction-limited volumes without a deformable mirror or wavefront sensor. Living human retina was imaged with visible nerve fibre layer, small capillary networks and photoreceptor cells. The authors note that the method also yields phase-sensitive volumes of other scattering samples at high speed.
