# In vivo functional imaging of human cone photoreceptors

**Jonnal et al. (2007)** · *Optics Express 15, 16141* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2709869/) · DOI: [10.1364/OE.15.016141](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.15.016141)

## Summary

Evaluates a non-invasive optical method for observing fast physiological processes associated with phototransduction in single human cones. The method relies on self-interference of multiple reflections within cone outer segments, which is sensitive to phase changes from refractive index and scattering variations. A 192 Hz AO flood-illumination camera recorded reflectance changes ('scintillation') in individual cones after visible stimulus flashes. The responses began within 5-10 ms, lasted 300-400 ms, were seen at visible and near-infrared wavelengths, and depended on the coherence length of the imaging source. The authors describe it as the first in vivo optical imaging of fast phototransduction-related signals in individual photoreceptors.
