# Imaging individual neurons in the retinal ganglion cell layer of the living eye

**Rossi et al. (2017)** · *PNAS 114, 586* · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5255596/) · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1613445114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1613445114)

## Summary

Shows that individual somas in the retinal ganglion cell layer, which are nearly transparent and invisible in confocal reflectance, can be imaged with a modification of confocal AOSLO that detects light at displaced (offset) apertures. Neurons were resolved in monkeys and humans, though human image quality was lower, partly because of light-safety limits. The same multi-offset approach applied to the photoreceptor layer resolved ambiguity about cone survival in age-related macular degeneration. The authors note potential uses in glaucoma research and in imaging other neural tissue.
