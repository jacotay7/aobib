# Adaptive optics on a 3.6-m telescope: results and performance

**Rigaut et al. (1991)** · *A&A 250, 280* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1991A%26A...250..280R)

## Summary

The paper reports the first observations with the COME-ON adaptive optics prototype on a large telescope, the ESO 3.6-m at La Silla, in April 1990. The system used a 5x5 Shack-Hartmann sensor and a 19-actuator deformable mirror with a separate tip-tilt mirror (about 20 degrees of freedom). The residual wavefront after correction is analysed with a Zernike expansion, and the corrected near-infrared images are used to evaluate angular-resolution gain and Strehl ratio, which approaches the diffraction limit down to 2.2 micron and reaches 0.6-0.8 at 3.8 micron. Short-exposure sequences are used to study temporal aspects of the correction, including the enhancement of the speckle modulation transfer function. The authors conclude with perspectives for AO in astronomy.
