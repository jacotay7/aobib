# A simple low-order adaptive optics system for near-infrared applications

**Roddier et al. (1991)** · *PASP 103, 131* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1991PASP..103..131R)

## Summary

The authors argue that low-order wavefront compensation can significantly improve near-infrared images over most of the sky, because fewer sensing areas means more photons per sample and fainter usable guide stars. They describe a novel approach optimised for low-order correction and high efficiency: curvature wavefront sensing combined with a curvature (bimorph-type) deformable mirror, whose response is naturally matched to the sensor signal. Computer simulations indicate the expected performance, and preliminary laboratory tests demonstrate feasibility. This system concept, developed at the University of Hawaii, led to the curvature AO systems later used at CFHT (PUEO), Gemini (Hokupa'a), Subaru, and ESO (MACAO).
