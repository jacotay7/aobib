# Rise of the machines: first year operations of the Robo-AO visible-light laser-adaptive optics instrument

**Baranec et al. (2013)** · *Proc. AMOS Technical Conference (Maui, 2013)* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.4432)

## Summary

Robo-AO is described as the first autonomous laser AO system and science instrument operating on sky. With minimal human oversight it executes large surveys, monitors long-term variability and characterises transients at the visible diffraction limit. The paper reports its first year at the Palomar 1.5-m telescope: live since June 2012, 78 science nights and more than 9000 robotic observations by August 2013, with an average target-to-target overhead of 86 s (up to about 20 observations per hour). Planned additions include a low-noise infrared camera also serving as a tip-tilt sensor, to extend wavelength coverage and sky coverage.
