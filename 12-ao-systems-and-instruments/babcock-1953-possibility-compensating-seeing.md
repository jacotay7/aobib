# The Possibility of Compensating Astronomical Seeing

**Babcock (1953)** · *PASP 65, 229* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1953PASP...65..229B)

## Summary

This short paper is the first published proposal of what is now called adaptive optics. Babcock notes that seeing on large telescopes appears mainly as image blurring caused by turbulent cells much smaller than the aperture, and that guiders can already remove overall image motion. He proposes measuring the local wavefront errors across the pupil with a schlieren-type (knife-edge) sensor viewed by an orthicon tube, and feeding the signal back to an "Eidophor" deformable oil-film element that applies a compensating phase correction in real time. The paper discusses the required response time, the brightness of the reference star, and the expected size of the corrected field. The concept of closed-loop compensation with a wavefront sensor and a deformable corrector introduced here is the basis of later AO systems.
