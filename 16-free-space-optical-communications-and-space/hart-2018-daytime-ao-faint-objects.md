# Extending Daytime Adaptive Optics to Faint Objects

**Hart (2018)** · *AMOS Technologies Conference 2018* · [Free full text](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2018/Adaptive-Optics_Imaging/Hart.pdf)

## Summary

Daytime imaging of satellites with adaptive optics on large telescopes is limited by sky background, which adds noise to or saturates the wavefront sensor. A sodium laser guide star observed through a magneto-optical filter can provide the high-order wavefront signal, but it does not measure overall image motion, which must come from the target itself and so sets a brightness limit in daylight. For fainter objects, laser-only correction gives nearly diffraction-limited images that wander randomly. The paper describes a registration algorithm that recovers high-resolution long exposures from a rapid series of very low signal-to-noise short exposures, using both past and future frames. The author expects it to allow daytime high-resolution imaging of objects as faint as about magnitude 12.
