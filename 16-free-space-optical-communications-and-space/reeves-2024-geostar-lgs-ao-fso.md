# GEOStar: demonstration of laser guide star adaptive optics for free space optical communications

**Reeves et al. (2024)** · *Proc. SPIE 12877, 128771S* · [Free full text](https://elib.dlr.de/220215/1/128771S-1.pdf) · DOI: [10.1117/12.3002594](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3002594)

## Summary

Adaptive optics at an optical ground station can pre-distort an uplink beam so that turbulence corrects it, but because of the point-ahead angle the downlink from the satellite is not a perfect wavefront reference. The GEOStar project aims to show that a laser guide star launched in the uplink direction gives a better reference for pre-distortion. In the setup, one sub-pupil of the 1-m ESA Optical Ground Station on Tenerife transmits the communication beam and a sub-pupil on the opposite side launches the LGS. An LGS wavefront sensor and a similar near-infrared wavefront sensor on the satellite downlink measure simultaneously so that the two can be compared directly, and a deformable mirror pre-distorts the uplink. The paper describes the design drivers, including LGS return flux and wavefront-sensor choice, and the system integration ahead of measurements with the TDP-1 terminal on Alphasat planned for 2024. The linked copy is the author version in the DLR electronic library.
