# The JPL Optical Communications Telescope Laboratory Test Bed for the Future Optical Deep Space Network

**Wilson et al. (2003)** · *IPN Progress Report 42-153* · [Free full text](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-153/153G.pdf)

## Summary

This report describes the Optical Communications Telescope Laboratory (OCTL), a 1-m research telescope that JPL was building at its Table Mountain Facility in California to develop ground-station strategies for laser communication from deep-space probes. The facility was designed as an optical antenna for both daytime and nighttime operation, including links at small Sun-Earth-probe angles. Planned early experiments included multiple-beam uplinks to retroreflector-carrying satellites to reduce the effect of atmospheric scintillation. The report also includes a preliminary analysis of how adaptive optics could improve daytime communication performance by allowing a smaller receiver field of view and so less sky background. OCTL was later used for the OPALS and LCRD ground stations and for the DSOC ground laser transmitter.
