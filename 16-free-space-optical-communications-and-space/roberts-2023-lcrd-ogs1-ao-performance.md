# Performance of the adaptive optics system for Laser Communications Relay Demonstration’s Ground Station 1

**Roberts et al. (2023)** · *Applied Optics 62, G26* · [Free full text](https://dataverse.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.48577/jpl.TY3IBB) · DOI: [10.1364/AO.486752](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.486752)

## Summary

NASA's Laser Communications Relay Demonstration (LCRD) is a multi-year demonstration of laser communication between the ground and a geosynchronous satellite, with optical ground stations in California (OGS1, at JPL's Table Mountain facility) and Hawaii. This paper describes the high-order AO system of OGS1, which compensates the downlink beam for atmospheric turbulence and couples it into the modem's single-mode fiber, with a requirement of 50% coupling efficiency. The system uses a woofer-tweeter pair of deformable mirrors and a Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor, and its real-time controller allows frame rates up to 20 kHz for the most demanding conditions. The authors describe the hardware and software design, the control system and the reduction of non-common-path aberrations, and present measured on-sky performance. The linked copy is the author manuscript in the JPL Open Repository.
