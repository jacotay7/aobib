# Daytime adaptive optics for deep space optical communication

**Roberts et al. (2019)** · *Proc. SPIE 11133, 1113308* · [Free full text](https://dataverse.jpl.nasa.gov/api/access/datafile/63980) · DOI: [10.1117/12.2524633](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2524633)

## Summary

Deep-space optical communication concepts usually rely on large (>8 m) ground telescopes with photon-counting detectors. In daytime, strong turbulence produces large point spread functions, so the detector needs a large field of view, which also admits more sky background and degrades the link, especially when the spacecraft is close to the Sun. The authors propose using adaptive optics to concentrate the light so that a smaller field of view can be used. They present an AO architecture designed to work in daytime with only moderate correction, give performance predictions for several telescope diameters, and list the technology gaps that need further development. The linked copy is the JPL Open Repository preprint.
