# Ground laser receivers for the Deep Space Optical Communications demonstration: performance and lessons learned from first year of operations

**Alerstam et al. (2025)** · *Proc. SPIE 13355, 133550N* · [Free full text](https://dataverse.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.48577/jpl.ORZETR) · DOI: [10.1117/12.3043323](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3043323)

## Summary

NASA's Deep Space Optical Communications (DSOC) demonstration, flown on the Psyche spacecraft, completed its first year of operations with photon-efficient downlinks from 163 kb/s to 267 Mb/s at ranges of 0.1 to 2.7 AU. This paper reports the performance of the primary ground laser receiver, installed on the 5-m Hale telescope at Palomar Observatory, and of a secondary receiver at JPL's 1-m OCTL telescope, which shares the aperture with the DSOC ground laser transmitter. The Palomar receiver couples the downlink onto a 64-channel superconducting nanowire single-photon detector array, with a fast steering mirror for tracking and zoom optics that set the detector field of view between 27 and 50 µrad. Palomar seeing-monitor data are compared with the field of view needed to capture 99 % of the downlink signal, showing that the minimum field of view rarely had to be increased. Lessons learned from operations are also discussed. The linked copy is the JPL Open Repository version.
