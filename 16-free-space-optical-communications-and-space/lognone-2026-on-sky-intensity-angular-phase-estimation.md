# The first on-sky demonstration of intensity-based angular phase estimation

**Lognoné et al. (2026)** · *Optics Express 34, 26309* · [Free full text](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.601796) · DOI: [10.1364/OE.601796](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.601796)

## Summary

Reports the first on-sky test of an MMSE estimator that uses both the on-axis phase and the on-axis intensity (log-amplitude) of a source, together with turbulence statistical priors, to estimate the phase in a nearby direction, as needed to pre-compensate a ground-to-satellite uplink at the point-ahead angle. Close visual binary stars separated by about 30 µrad were observed in the visible with an open-loop Shack-Hartmann sensor, whose subaperture slopes and fluxes gave the phase and log-amplitude measurements. The authors report up to 28 % reduction in anisoplanatic tip errors compared with phase-only estimation, in agreement with theory, and model photon and detector noise and the light of the companion star. It is the experimental follow-up of the phase and log-amplitude point-ahead estimator of Lognoné et al. (2023, Optics Express 31, 3441).
