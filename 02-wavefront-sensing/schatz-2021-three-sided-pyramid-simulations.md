# Three-sided pyramid wavefront sensor, part 1: simulations and analysis for astronomical adaptive optics

**Schatz et al. (2021)** · *JATIS 7, 049001* · [Free full text](https://hal.science/hal-03512484) · DOI: [10.1117/1.JATIS.7.4.049001](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JATIS.7.4.049001)

## Summary

Studies the three-sided pyramid wavefront sensor (3PWFS), which forms three pupil images instead of the four of the conventional pyramid and therefore uses fewer detector pixels, as an option for extreme AO on giant segmented mirror telescopes. A formalism based on the diffraction theory of the Foucault knife-edge test predicts the intensity in the pupil images for arbitrary Fourier-mode phase errors and is used to motivate the signal processing, including a slopes-maps calculation adapted to three pupils and a raw-intensity approach. End-to-end OOMAO simulations of a modulated pyramid compare the 3PWFS and the four-sided sensor. With a low-read-noise detector the Strehl ratios agree within 0.01; with higher read noise at stellar magnitude 10, the 3PWFS with raw intensities gains 0.036 in Strehl over the four-sided sensor with slopes maps. The authors conclude that the 3PWFS can reconstruct the wavefront and close a stable loop with correction comparable to the four-sided pyramid.
