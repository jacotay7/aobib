# Première mondiale de l'optique adaptative en astronomie en 1989 (World first of adaptive optics in astronomy in 1989)

**Fontanella et al. (2021)** · *Photoniques 111, 33-36* · [Free full text](https://insu.hal.science/insu-03705259) · DOI: [10.1051/photon/202111133](https://doi.org/10.1051/photon/202111133)

## Summary

In this short historical article (in French), members of the original team recount the first demonstration of adaptive optics for astronomy in 1989, when the COME-ON prototype showed that the effects of atmospheric turbulence could be compensated to reach the diffraction limit of a large telescope, at least in the near infrared. The authors describe how the technology, possibly combined with laser guide stars, became an essential part of all large ground-based optical telescopes. They also note that the observations behind the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics relied first on speckle imaging and then on AO and its application to multi-telescope interferometry.
