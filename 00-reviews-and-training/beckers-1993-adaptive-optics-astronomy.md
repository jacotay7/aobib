# Adaptive Optics for Astronomy: Principles, Performance, and Applications

**Beckers (1993)** · *ARA&A 31, 13-62* · [Free full text](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1993ARA%26A..31...13B) · DOI: [10.1146/annurev.aa.31.090193.000305](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.aa.31.090193.000305)

## Summary

This Annual Review article by J. M. Beckers (ESO) surveys adaptive optics for astronomy at the time the first astronomical AO systems were entering operation. It introduces the principle of compensating atmospheric wavefront distortions with a controllable mirror driven by a wavefront sensor using natural or laser guide stars, and defines the terminology, including the distinction between active optics (slow, telescope-induced errors) and adaptive optics (10-1000 Hz atmospheric correction). The review then discusses the performance expected from AO and its limitations, the hardware components, and astronomical applications. The 50-page article is available as a full scan through NASA ADS and is a reference point for the state of the field in the early 1990s.
