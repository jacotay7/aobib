# Adaptive optics in high-contrast imaging

**Milli et al. (2016)** · *Astronomy at High Angular Resolution, ASSL 439, p. 17 (Springer)* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.00836) · DOI: [10.1007/978-3-319-39739-9_2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-39739-9_2)

## Summary

This book chapter reviews the use of adaptive optics for high-contrast imaging, that is, imaging the close vicinity of bright stars to detect companions and circumstellar material at contrasts below about 1e-4. After introducing the science case and the technical requirements, it reviews the architecture of standard and extreme AO systems across three generations of instruments, from the pioneering experiments of the 1990s through the first 8-m-class instruments of the 2000s to the extreme AO systems that began operating in the mid-2010s. It concludes with selected science highlights on protoplanetary disks, young giant planets and debris disks.
