# Introduction to Adaptive Optics and its History

**Max (2001)** · *Talk text, 197th AAS Meeting, San Diego (January 2001)* · [Free full text](https://www.ucolick.org/~max/History_AO_Max.htm)

## Summary

Web version of an invited presentation given by Claire Max at the 197th meeting of the American Astronomical Society (2001). It explains for a general astronomical audience why AO is needed, how turbulence blurs images, and how wavefront sensors, deformable mirrors and laser guide stars are combined to correct it. A section on the heritage of the technology traces the astronomical and military threads: Babcock's 1953 proposal, the first significant systems built by the military and aerospace communities from the late 1960s (Starfire Optical Range, AEOS on Maui), an early one-dimensional correction experiment by Luis Alvarez's group, and early theoretical work by Dyson, Roddier and Hardy. It also contrasts military and astronomical requirements and lists the key components of an AO system.
