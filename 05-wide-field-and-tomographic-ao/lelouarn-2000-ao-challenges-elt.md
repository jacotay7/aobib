# New challenges for adaptive optics: extremely large telescopes

**Le Louarn et al. (2000)** · *MNRAS 317, 535* · [Free full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0004065) · DOI: [10.1046/j.1365-8711.2000.03607.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-8711.2000.03607.x)

## Summary

The paper computes analytically the performance of an AO system on a 100 m telescope working in the visible. A 60% Strehl ratio at 0.5 um is reachable with guide stars near R~10, but with very low sky coverage. To address this, tomographic wavefront sensing over a wider field with three or four natural or laser guide stars and up to three deformable mirrors is investigated, increasing the corrected field up to eight-fold. With natural guide stars the approach is restricted to the infrared, giving about 50% sky coverage at Strehl 0.2 in J band, while the laser guide star option with a faint natural star for low orders gives about 40% sky coverage at the Galactic poles in the visible.
