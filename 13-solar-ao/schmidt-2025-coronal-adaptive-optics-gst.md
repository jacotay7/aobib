# Observations of fine coronal structures with high-order solar adaptive optics

**Schmidt et al. (2025)** · *Nature Astronomy 9, 1148* · [Free full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-025-02564-0) · DOI: [10.1038/s41550-025-02564-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-025-02564-0)

## Summary

Presents observations with a coronal adaptive optics system at the 1.6-m Goode Solar Telescope in Big Bear that reaches the telescope diffraction limit on off-limb coronal targets. Conventional solar AO systems lock on photospheric granulation and cannot correct observations of faint coronal structures above the limb. The authors report a roughly tenfold increase in spatial resolution at visible wavelengths, with coronal rain material resolved at scales below 100 km, and a short-lived, fast-moving twisted feature observed during the decay phase of a flare. The paper positions coronal AO as a route to diffraction-limited studies of coronal dynamics with large ground-based telescopes.
